Distributed transactions: Saga and Outbox

One order touches three services, each with its own database. A saga runs one local transaction per service and undoes earlier steps if a later one fails. The outbox makes sure "save the data" and "publish the event" never drift apart. Below the lab, see why 2PC blocks.

Try it: two-phase commit under failure

Say this in the interview

"Order, payment and inventory each own their data, so there's no single ACID transaction, and the PSP won't join a 2PC. I'll use an orchestrated saga: each step is a local transaction with a compensation, run in reverse on failure; the charge is the pivot. Every service writes its event to an outbox in the same transaction, and a relay or Debezium publishes it. Delivery is at-least-once, so every handler dedupes on event id."

Rules for good compensations

  • A compensation is a new action ("refund", "release"), not a rollback. The original write stays in history.
  • Idempotent and retried until it succeeds. If it keeps failing: NEEDS_ATTENTION + ops queue.
  • Undoable steps before the pivot (charge); retry-forward steps after it; email and shipping last.
  • No isolation: use PENDING states and reservations with a TTL.

Check yourself

Why not publish to Kafka first, then write the DB?
Same bug in reverse: if the DB write fails after publishing, consumers act on an order that does not exist. Any two separate writes can half-fail. The outbox turns them into one local write.
The orchestrator crashes mid-saga. What happens?
It saved the saga state after every step. On restart it reads that state and continues forward or keeps compensating. Steps are idempotent, so repeating the last one is safe.
Polling the outbox vs CDC?
Polling (SELECT unsent rows every 100 ms with SKIP LOCKED) is simple but adds DB load and delay. CDC (Debezium on the WAL or binlog) has tens-of-ms delay and keeps commit order, but it is one more system to run.
Why can't an in-doubt 2PC participant just abort?
The coordinator may already have logged COMMIT and told the other participant to commit. Aborting alone would break atomicity, so it must wait with its locks held.
