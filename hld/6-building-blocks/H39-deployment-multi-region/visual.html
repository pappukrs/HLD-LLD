Deployment, autoscaling and multi-region

Three ways a system survives change and load: a canary that stops a bad release at 10%, an autoscaler that is always a minute late unless you pre-scale, and a warm standby region whose replication lag is your RPO.

v1 / primary / capacity Canary / booting / traffic Healthy / promoted Bad version / overload / outage

Say this in the interview

"Deploys are canaries: 1%, 10%, 50%, 100%, with automatic rollback if errors, p99 or payment success get worse than baseline. Pods scale on in-flight requests and queue lag, and we pre-scale before known sale events because pods take a minute to boot. We run 3 AZs in Mumbai with a warm standby in Hyderabad: async replication gives an RPO of a few seconds, and a rehearsed runbook that fences the old primary gives an RTO under 15 minutes. Both regions are in India for RBI data localisation."

Check yourself

Why did reactive scaling still drop requests?
CPU is capped at 100%, so each HPA round can only ask for current × 100/60 pods, and each round waits about a minute for pods to boot. It takes three rounds to catch a 4x spike.
Why scale down slowly?
Traffic is bumpy. Scaling down on every dip and back up on every bump (flapping) wastes boots and risks being short when the next bump comes. HPA waits 5 minutes by default.
What is the RPO with 3 s async lag?
About 3 seconds of writes. With 800 writes/s that is roughly 2,400 writes that may need recovery from the old region.
Why fence the old primary before promoting?
If the old region comes back and still accepts writes, both regions are primary (split brain) and data diverges. Fencing revokes its write access first.

Numbers

  • Rolling defaults: maxSurge 25%, maxUnavailable 25%
  • HPA checks every 15 s; scale-down window 5 min
  • Pod boot 30 to 90 s; new node 1 to 3 min
  • Mumbai to Hyderabad ~15 to 25 ms round trip
  • 99.99% = 53 min of downtime a year
