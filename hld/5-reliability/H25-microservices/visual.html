Microservices and Service Discovery

Split by business capability, give each service its own database, and put a gateway in front. Then the new problem: instances come and go, so how does Order find a live Payment instance? Watch registration, client-side and server-side discovery, a crash, and why sync chains cost availability.

Say this in the interview

"I split by business capability, each service with its own database. Clients enter through an API gateway that does TLS, JWT checks, rate limiting and request ids. Services find each other through Kubernetes DNS; a pod joins the endpoints only after its readiness probe passes. Checkout makes sync calls only where it needs the answer, each with a timeout and breaker. Everything else is async over Kafka."

Signs of a distributed monolith

  • Services share a database or read each other's tables.
  • Two services must always be deployed together.
  • One user request walks a sync chain of 5+ services.
  • Business logic lives in the gateway.

Check yourself

Why not hard-code Payment's IP addresses?
Pods are replaced on every deploy, crash and autoscale. Their IPs change within minutes. Discovery keeps the list current.
What removes a dead instance from the registry?
Missed heartbeats past a TTL (Eureka, Consul) or a failing readiness probe / deleted pod (Kubernetes endpoints).
5 sync services at 99.9% each. Combined availability?
0.999^5 ≈ 99.5%, about 3.6 hours down a month instead of 43 minutes.
Why make the receipt email async?
Checkout does not need its answer. With an event on Kafka, a slow or broken notification service cannot slow down or fail checkout.
