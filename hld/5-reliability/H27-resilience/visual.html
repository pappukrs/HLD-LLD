Timeouts, Retries, Circuit Breakers, Bulkheads

When a dependency is down, waiting on it wastes threads and time, and retrying all at once can knock it down again. A circuit breaker stops calls to a failing service. Backoff with jitter spreads retries out so the service can recover. A bulkhead caps how many calls one slow dependency may hold, so the others keep working.

Say this in the interview

"Every call to Payment has a timeout. I wrap it in a circuit breaker: if more than 50% of the last 20 calls fail, it opens and we fail fast with a fallback. After 30 seconds it lets one probe through. I retry only idempotent calls, at most 3 times, with exponential backoff and full jitter, so clients don't retry in sync and cause a thundering herd."

Rules that keep retries safe

  • Retry only idempotent operations, or send an idempotency key with the request.
  • Retry only errors that may pass: timeouts, 503, 429. Never retry a 400.
  • Cap the attempts and use a retry budget (for example, retries ≤ 10% of all requests).
  • Retry at one layer only. Three layers × 3 retries = 27 calls for one user request.
  • Respect Retry-After when the server sends it.

Check yourself

Why is a timeout alone not enough?
With a 2 s timeout, every call to a dead service still holds a connection or worker for 2 s. Under load the caller's pool fills up and the caller fails too. The breaker turns those 2 s waits into instant failures.
What does the caller return when the breaker is open?
A fallback: cached data, a default value (for example "recommendations unavailable"), a queued request for later, or a clear 503. Pick per feature. Payments usually fail clearly; a recommendation widget can just hide.
Why let only one probe through in half-open?
The service may still be weak. If all traffic came back at once it could fall over again. Some libraries allow a few probes and close only after N successes.
Count failures or a failure rate?
A rate over a sliding window (for example 50% of the last 20 calls, with a minimum number of calls) works better. A plain count trips too easily at high traffic and too slowly at low traffic.

More checks

Three layers each retry 3 times. One call fails at the bottom. How many calls hit the bottom service?
3 × 3 × 3 = 27. Retry in one layer only, and keep a retry budget (about 10% of traffic).
How do you build a bulkhead in Node, which has no thread pools?
A semaphore per downstream (p-limit or a cockatiel bulkhead), a separate http.Agent with maxSockets per host, and separate DB pools for slow work.
