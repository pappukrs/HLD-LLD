Observability: Traces, Logs, Metrics, SLOs

Follow one slow checkout through four services. The trace shows where the time went, the logs with the same trace id show why, the histogram shows how many users felt it, and the error budget decides whether someone gets paged.

Try it 1.50%
Span / trace context Fast / healthy Slow / warning Error / alert

Say this in the interview

"Every service exports RED metrics with latency as histograms, plus business metrics like payment success per PSP. Traces use OpenTelemetry with the traceparent header, carried through Kafka headers, with tail sampling that keeps all errors and slow traces. Logs are JSON with the trace id and no card data. We page on SLO burn rate, 14.4× over an hour, not on CPU."

What goes where

  • Metric label: route, status, method, region, pod. Bounded values only.
  • Trace attribute / log field: user_id, order_id, payment_id, bank response code.
  • Never anywhere: card number, CVV, OTP, password, raw tokens.

Check yourself

Average latency is 130 ms. Is the service fine?
Maybe not. In step 5 the average is fine while p99 is 2 s. With 20 calls per page, many page loads hit at least one slow call. Look at p95/p99.
Why sum histograms instead of averaging p99s?
A quiet pod with a high p99 skews the average. Bucket counts add up across pods; the percentile from the merged buckets is the real fleet p99.
30 minutes of full outage against a 99.9% / 30-day SLO. Budget left?
43.2 − 30 = 13.2 minutes, about 31%. Slow down risky releases.
Why alert on burn rate, not on "error rate > 1%"?
Burn rate ties the alert to the SLO: it pages for fast burns that threaten the month, ignores short blips, and the same rule works for any SLO target.
