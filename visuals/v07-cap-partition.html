CAP Under a Network Partition

Networks do break. When nodes cannot talk, a replicated system must choose: refuse some requests to stay correct (CP), or answer every request and fix differences later (AP). Without a partition you get both.

Write / replication Success Rejected / cut link Diverged data

Say this in the interview

"Partitions will happen, so the real choice is what to do during one. For payments and inventory I choose CP: the minority side rejects writes, and users retry. For a shopping cart, likes or a feed I choose AP: every side accepts writes and we merge later with version vectors or CRDTs. Even with no partition there is a trade-off between latency and consistency: that is PACELC."

Common misreadings

  • "Pick 2 of 3" is wrong: you cannot give up P. You pick C or A while partitioned.
  • CAP's C is linearizability, not the C in ACID.
  • CAP's A means every non-failed node answers. A CP system is still usually up; only the minority side refuses.
  • Many systems are tunable per request (Cassandra consistency levels, DynamoDB strongly consistent reads).

Check yourself

Why can't a 2-node cluster stay CP if the link breaks?
Neither side has a majority (2 of 2 needed), so a CP system must stop writes on both sides. That is why consensus clusters use 3 or 5 nodes: one side always can have a majority.
What does last-write-wins lose?
Concurrent updates. If two sides add different items to a cart, LWW keeps one write and silently drops the other. Clock skew can even make an older write "win". Merging (union, CRDT) keeps both.
What is PACELC?
If Partition: choose Availability or Consistency. Else (normal operation): choose Latency or Consistency. Waiting for remote replicas makes reads correct but slower. DynamoDB and Cassandra are PA/EL; Spanner is PC/EC.
