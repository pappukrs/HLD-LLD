Feed Fan-out: Push vs Pull

A home feed shows recent posts from everyone you follow. You can do the work when a post is written (push), when the feed is read (pull), or mix both so celebrities do not melt your write path.

Try it: cost of one post and one feed open

Say this in the interview

"Reads are about 100× more than writes, so I'll fan out on write: when a user posts, workers push the post id into a Redis list for each follower, and reading a feed is one list read plus a multi-get of posts. For accounts above about 10K followers I skip the fan-out and pull their recent posts at read time, then merge. I keep only the last ~800 ids per user and skip inactive users."

Numbers to know

  • An id list of 800 × 8-byte ids is about 6.4 KB per user. 100M active users ≈ 640 GB of Redis, so shard it by user id.
  • Average follower count is small (hundreds), but the max is huge (100M+). Design for the tail, not the average.
  • Fan-out is async through a queue. A few seconds of feed delay is fine; a slow post button is not.
  • Store ids in the timeline, not post bodies. Edits and deletes then only touch one place.

Check yourself

A user deletes a post that was pushed to 2,000 timelines. What do you do?
Do not rush to clean 2,000 lists. Mark the post deleted in the posts store; hydration drops ids whose post is missing or deleted. Clean the lists lazily or in a background job.
Bob follows a new account. Does his feed update?
Push only covers future posts. Either pull that account's recent posts once and merge them into Bob's list, or accept that only new posts appear.
Why skip inactive users?
Most of your users do not open the app every day. Pushing to them wastes writes and memory. When they come back, rebuild the timeline with a pull, which is slow once and then cached.
Where does ranking fit?
Push or pull gives you a candidate set (a few hundred ids). A ranking service then scores and reorders them before the response. Chronological feeds skip this step.
