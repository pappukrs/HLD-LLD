Chain of Responsibility: who stops the request?

A payment request walks down a line of handlers: auth, rate limit, amount, fraud, then the charge. Each link either rejects and stops, or calls next(). Then the same idea as GoF objects: an ATM where each note cassette takes what it can and passes the rest.

    Say this in the interview

    "The checks before a payment are a chain of responsibility. Each link has one job and either rejects with a clear error or calls next. It is the same model as Express middleware and NestJS guards. Each endpoint composes its own chain from the same links, cheap checks first and the slow fraud call last."

    Check yourself

    Why put the fraud check last?
    It costs around 50 ms and a network call. Cheap checks (auth, rate limit, amount) reject most bad requests first, so fraud runs only for requests that might succeed.
    What happens in Express if a middleware neither responds nor calls next()?
    The request hangs until a timeout. Every link must either answer or pass on.
    The ATM has notes left for part of ₹1,100 but not all. What should it do?
    Plan the whole amount first; if any remainder falls off the end of the chain, reject and remove no notes.
    How do you test one link?
    Call it with a fake next and assert whether next was called and what it returned. No need to build the whole chain.
