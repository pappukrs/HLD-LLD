A refund job crashes on a gift card, a square breaks a rectangle, and a payment service cannot be tested without Postgres. Three principles fix three real failures: keep subtypes honest, keep interfaces small, and plug details in from outside.

The three principles: smell and fix

PrincipleRuleSmell you can seeFix
L: Liskov substitutionA subtype keeps every promise of its parentSubclass throws "not supported", no-op overrides, callers use instanceofPromise only what all subtypes can do; split the interface or compose
I: Interface segregationClients depend only on methods they useClasses implement methods they cannot honour; fat IServiceSmall role interfaces: Payable, Refundable
D: Dependency inversionPolicy and details both depend on abstractions owned by the policynew PostgresRepo() inside business logic; tests need a real DBConstructor injection; one composition root

Check yourself

Does compiling mean LSP holds?
No. TypeScript checks method names and types. LSP is about behaviour: no new exceptions, no stronger input rules, no weaker results, invariants kept.
Why did Square give area 16?
setHeight(4) also set the width to 4. Code written for Rectangle expects the two to be independent, so it expected 5 × 4 = 20.
Who owns PaymentRepo?
The domain, next to PaymentService, in the words the service needs. Postgres implements it. The arrow from the detail points up to the abstraction: that is the inversion.
DIP or DI?
DIP is the principle; DI (passing dependencies in) is the technique; a container like NestJS is a tool for DI.
